# Demo dashboard to LUMS hackathon judging narrative

| Pitch question | Dashboard evidence and demonstration |
|---|---|
| What is the problem? | Header and hero: energy at the wrong time/place; flexible demand, grid limits and rider downtime |
| What is the solution? | One inventory, three delivery modes; mobile truck, DC–DC site BESS and compatible rider swapping |
| What does energy contribute? | Source mix, input/output/losses, mode allocation and dispatch ledger |
| Can this earn money? | Financial contribution after modeled acquisition, wear, operating, route and site costs; NOT a claimed net profit |
| Is it environmentally better? | Transparent operational baseline vs charging emissions, adjustable attribution fraction and solar share; does not assert an LCA |
| How will the pilot be judged? | Six pilot outcomes, 0–30 / 31–60 / 61–90 day roadmap, safety and go/no-go gating |

## Numbers from the presenter brief

- **Energy spread example:** buy 100 kWh × Rs 40 = Rs 4,000. Sell 85 kWh × Rs 105 = Rs 8,925. Gross energy-only spread Rs 4,925 before all other costs.
- **Car operational example:** 100,000 attributable displaced petrol kilometres, 10 km/litre; 0.15 kWh/km EV; 85% delivered efficiency; 0.365 kgCO2/kWh grid; 2.31 kgCO2/litre. Approximately 16.7 tCO2 operationally avoided using grid supply; 19.9 tCO2 with 50% hypothetical verified solar.
- **Rider example:** 20 riders × 40 km/day × 300 days = 240,000 km; modeled 9.2 tCO2 with grid supply or 11.5 tCO2 with 50% solar, at full petrol displacement.

**Every result above is hypothetical; none is actual Bijli Haazir pilot performance.**
